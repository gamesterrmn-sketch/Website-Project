THE CHALLENGE

One event should not define an entire evaluation.

A recent or memorable concern can influence an overall score. The proposed approach distinguishes general service experience from isolated concerns, recurring deficiencies, SLA failures, and critical incidents.

◉

Clear behavioral anchors

Ratings 5 to 1 describe observable performance across the full evaluation period.

▣

Verified evidence

Operational records, inspections, staffing, maintenance, response times, and compliance support the rating.

⚑

Critical-gate protection

A verified serious safety, legal, security, food-safety, fraud, or ethical failure cannot be hidden by an arithmetic average.

RECOMMENDED ARCHITECTURE

Measure what matters, using the right evidence.

Objective operational performance50%
Safety, legal, and contractual compliance20%
Employee and service-user experience20%
Governance and continuous improvement10%

Core principles

  • Listen: Preserve employee feedback on visible service quality.
  • Verify: Connect conclusions to authorized operational evidence.
  • Classify: Separate isolated, recurring, SLA, major, and critical concerns.
  • Improve: Track corrective action and prevent recurrence.
Important: Suggested weights are starting points and should be calibrated against service risk and contract requirements.
PROPORTIONATE RESPONSE

Classify the concern before changing the score.

Isolated minor

Record, verify, correct, and monitor.

Recurring deficiency

Root-cause analysis and score impact.

SLA failure

Apply the KPI formula and contract response.

Major incident

Investigate formally and escalate.

Critical incident

Contain immediately and apply critical-gate review.
